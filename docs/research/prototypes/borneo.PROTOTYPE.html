IGNIS

Integrated Geospatial Network for Ignition Surveillance · an entry to the NASA Space Apps Challenge 2026, “MODIS and VIIRS Hot Spots” (Sarawak)

IGNIS helps people prepare for Borneo’s next fire season by showing, in plain words, when and where fires have burned over 20+ years, and where this year stands against the usual.

  • One consistent count. Two generations of NASA satellites see fire differently. IGNIS puts both on one scale, the H Count, so 2005 and 2025 can be compared fairly.
  • Seasons and busy years. The Burning Calendar shows which months usually burn and which years burned most.
  • Compared with the usual. Check whether any month or area is burning more or less than it normally does.
  • How fires move. Hotspot Shifts follow a fire from one satellite pass to the next, about 12 hours apart.
  • Take it with you. Download maps, a printable report, spreadsheets and the raw hotspots (CSV).

  • Fire responders and firefighters: see where fires are spreading and when the busy weeks usually start.
  • Land managers: check whether their land burns more than usual, and in which months.
  • Local authorities: plan patrols, warnings and resources ahead of the fire season.
  • Early-warning teams: spot months running well above the usual, early.
  • Scientists: study 20+ years of fire on one consistent scale, and download the data.

  • NASA FIRMS (Fire Information for Resource Management System), part of NASA’s Earth Science Data and Information System (ESDIS): active-fire detections from the archive download, three records:
    • MODIS Collection 6.1 on the Terra and Aqua satellites, about 1 km pixels, 2002 onward.
    • VIIRS on Suomi NPP (S-NPP), 375 m pixels, 2012 onward.
    • VIIRS on NOAA-20, 375 m pixels, 2018 onward.
    Each Hotspot carries its time, confidence and Fire Radiative Power (FRP).
  • geoBoundaries (William & Mary geoLab, open licence CC BY 4.0): state, province and district boundaries. Runfola, D. et al. (2020), PLOS ONE 15(4): e0231866.
  • Esri ArcGIS Online: World Dark Gray and World Light Gray Canvas basemaps, and World Imagery for the satellite view (Esri and its data partners, including Maxar and Earthstar Geographics).
  • Leaflet 1.9.4 (BSD-2-Clause): the interactive map.
  • ExcelJS 4.4.0 (MIT): spreadsheet downloads.
  • NASA Space Apps Challenge 2026: the “MODIS and VIIRS Hot Spots” challenge this entry answers.

We acknowledge the use of data and imagery from NASA’s Fire Information for Resource Management System (FIRMS), part of NASA’s Earth Science Data and Information System (ESDIS). NASA does not endorse IGNIS. Hotspots are satellite detections of heat, not confirmed fires on the ground, and some fires are missed under cloud or smoke.

IGNIS was built with help from AI coding assistants: Claude Code, Codex and Antigravity. Every number comes from NASA satellite data, and no images in the app are AI-generated.
