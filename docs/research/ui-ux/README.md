## Answers to open questions

The five open questions from section 7 of the plan, each with a concrete recommendation and the evidence behind it. Pattern names refer to [PATTERNS.md](PATTERNS.md).

### 1. Map-first or calendar-first landing view?

**Recommendation: map-first — land on the heatmap over the Borneo Coverage Area with the Burning Calendar server-rendered beside it, linked, not on a separate tab.**

Evidence: every responder-grade reference is map-first with live data already drawn — FIRMS loads the last 24 hours of Hotspots on the map ([nasa-firms.md](nasa-firms.md) checklist #1), Watch Duty reaches a useful answer at ≈zero clicks ([watch-duty.md](watch-duty.md) #1), Windy and ASMC follow the same shape. Meanwhile GitHub proves the calendar half can ship in the first HTML paint with zero config ([github-contrib-graph.md](github-contrib-graph.md) #1, #13). The anti-evidence is equally clear: GNW ex-GFW buries fire two menus under three default forest layers (~11 clicks to the first fire answer) and Windy hides its fire layer ~90 % down settings — a flagship view that is not on the first screen does not get used. Co-locating when and where follows Climate Reanalyzer's chart-plus-map page. No audited app links a calendar and a map in both directions — that contract is IGNIS's own, and PATTERNS.md's Ground rules now specify it: clicking a calendar cell or a recent-days strip day filters the heatmap to that period; selecting an Area of Interest or a date on the heatmap recomputes and highlights the Burning Calendar; both share one URL state. On a phone the calendar lives in a bottom-sheet drawer with the recent-days strip always visible (per the *Mobile-first shell* pattern). Both views must land inside the Ground-rules performance budget — ≤ 3 s and ≤ 1 MB to first useful answer — the line FIRMS (0.5 MB) and SiPongi+ (~2.8 s) meet and GNW (12.5 s / 2 MB), ASMC (~18 s / 2.4 MB) and Worldview (15.6 s / 6.5 MB) fail. Patterns: *Map-first landing over the Coverage Area*, *Year-by-month grid under a Typical Year*, *Flagship data one click from the top*, *Recent-days strip*, *Mobile-first shell with bottom-sheet drawers*.

### 2. Show raw MODIS and VIIRS layers, or only the Harmonized Hotspot Count?

**Recommendation: both — the Harmonized Hotspot Count is the default headline number and default layer, with per-sensor MODIS/VIIRS counts always visible beside it and raw per-sensor layers one toggle away, each with a plain-language capability label.**

Evidence: FIRMS merges everything into one layer with no per-sensor toggles and its audit calls that its biggest gap ([nasa-firms.md](nasa-firms.md) #5); SiPongi+ and Watch Duty never merge at all — honest, but leaves no comparable series across the record, which is why GWIS can only juxtapose MODIS and VIIRS charts instead of reconciling them. GitHub shows the shape that works: one count in the cell, per-source breakdown one click down, method link under the grid. What the headline number *is* has been settled in the repo: the [harmonization analysis](../docs/research/harmonization-analysis.md) and [ADR 0001](../docs/adr/0001-viirs-equivalent-scaling.md) define the Harmonized Hotspot Count as VIIRS-equivalent counts — observed VIIRS from 2012, MODIS-only 2002–2011 months scaled by per-month factors at ±12% — and the app should link straight to them wherever the number appears. Patterns: *Per-sensor counts beside the Harmonized Hotspot Count* (with the disagreement resolved toward GitHub's model), *Sensor-explicit layers with capability one-liners and freshness stamps*, *Read-then-drill ladder*, *Methodology one click from every number*.

### 3. Calendar granularity: daily, weekly or monthly cells?

**Recommendation: monthly cells by default, with one-click re-binning radios to weekly and daily for drill-down.**

Evidence from the audited apps first: GWIS's month×year fire-season heatmap is the working ancestor — a whole multi-decade record reads in one glance ([gwis.md](gwis.md) walkthrough task 2), and monthly is the finest granularity that keeps a 25-year harmonized record (2002–present, per the [harmonization analysis](../docs/research/harmonization-analysis.md)) on one screen. GitHub's daily grid works only because a year fits 53 slim columns and it still offers no aggregation ([github-contrib-graph.md](github-contrib-graph.md)); ASMC's monthly charts answer the same question but cost 5 clicks per year because nothing is linked ([asmc.md](asmc.md) task 2); the D3 notebooks prove re-binning is a one-click radio, not a zoom ([d3-calendar.md](d3-calendar.md) — facet radios rebuilt the chart in one verified click). GLOSSARY.md's definition of the Burning Calendar as a year-by-month grid is the team's codified conclusion from this same evidence, not independent support for it. Daily detail still matters for responders, delivered two ways: the drill-down (GitHub's cell-click filters a detail list) and the *Recent-days strip* pattern now in PATTERNS.md (APIMS's hourly chip table, ASMC's 10-day chips, Windy's day strip). Patterns: *Year-by-month grid under a Typical Year*, *One-click re-binning radios*, *Read-then-drill ladder*, *Recent-days strip*.

### 4. Do we need a Sarawak haze/impact view, or only burning in the Coverage Area?

**Recommendation: yes to haze impact, as a panel inside the region panel rather than a separate view — current air-quality tier plus health actions beside the Harmonized Hotspot Count, anchored to the Typical Year.**

Evidence: the plan's own audience list includes the haze-impact audience — defined in PATTERNS.md as everyone in the Coverage Area exposed to haze, downwind Sarawak/Kuching and source-side Kalimantan alike — and the audit set shows exactly how to serve them: IQAir's Kuching page answers "how bad is the air" in 2 clicks with tier, WHO context and an action ([apims-iqair.md](apims-iqair.md) task 1); APIMS's tier cards answer "how bad overall, where do I sit" in 0 clicks; ASMC pairs its map with a daily prose narrative translating Hotspots into haze and air quality, plus wind/rain overlays. IQAir's map also demonstrates cause and consequence on one canvas ("Fires" beside "Wind" and air-quality stations). Keeping it a panel — Burning Phase tier cards + AQI tier + health action + a "3× a Typical March" anchor — serves that audience without fragmenting the app the way ASMC's separate portal pages fragment theirs. One dependency is open and named as such in the pattern: no audited source exposes a free, downloadable Sarawak/Kuching air-quality feed (APIMS's pages are static with no export; IQAir publishes no public CSV; ASMC's WIS2 catalog covers Hotspot coordinates, not AQ), so which feed ships — APIMS/MyEQMS, IQAir, or an ASMC tie-in — is an architect decision, and until it is settled the panel links out rather than fabricating a tier. Patterns: *Haze-impact panel with health actions and a Typical Year anchor*, *Burning Phase tier cards with counts and ranges*, *One tabbed context-overlay panel*.

### 5. Which languages ship first?

**Recommendation: ship one string table at launch with English as the default plus Bahasa Indonesia and Bahasa Malaysia behind a one-click toggle in the header.**

Evidence: SiPongi+ proves a complete ID/EN toggle translating every string (nav, filters, dates, admin levels) is achievable for exactly this audience ([sipongi.md](sipongi.md) #12 — verdict inherit); GNW ex-GFW ships six languages including Bahasa Indonesia and its audit marks that inherit; APIMS publishes its haze advisory in Bahasa Malaysia, showing the demand on the Sarawak side. The avoid-side evidence: FIRMS, ASMC, GWIS, Worldview and Copernicus Browser are all English-only despite serving Borneo-adjacent audiences — GNW and SiPongi+ are the only Tier-1 apps that meet their audience in its own language. The Coverage Area spans Bahasa Indonesia-speaking Kalimantan (including the source-side residents the haze-impact audience definition covers) and Bahasa Malaysia-speaking Sarawak, Sabah and Brunei, so EN+ID+MS at launch is the minimum honest scope; the *combination* is IGNIS's own, so the pattern's verdict is adapt (mechanism inherited from SiPongi+, scope added). Pattern: *English + Bahasa Indonesia + Bahasa Malaysia from day one, Iban next*.

Iban is a team addition, not something the audits found: it is the language of Sarawak's largest ethnic group and reaches rural and longhouse communities near recurring burning. No audited app ships it and the fire terms lack settled Iban wording, so build the string table for a fourth locale now and ship Iban at launch only if a native-speaker reviewer checks the strings; otherwise it is the first post-launch locale.
