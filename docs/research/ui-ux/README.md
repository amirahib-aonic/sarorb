## Answers to open questions

The five open questions from section 7 of the plan plus the three Borneo supplement questions (Q1–Q3 from [the supplement brief](../UI-UX-BORNEO-SUPPLEMENT.md) section 6) — eight answers, each with a concrete recommendation and the evidence behind it. Pattern names refer to [PATTERNS.md](PATTERNS.md). Open data dependencies that gate several answers (tri-country boundary GIS, MY/BN admin codes, the harmonization refit, AQ and FDRS feeds, the endonym gazetteer, the Iban reviewer) are registered in one list at the end of PATTERNS.md — named, unowned by the UI, and not to be read as settled.

### 1. Map-first or calendar-first landing view?

**Recommendation: map-first — land on the heatmap over the Borneo Coverage Area with the Burning Calendar server-rendered beside it, linked, not on a separate tab.**

Evidence: every responder-grade reference is map-first with live data already drawn — FIRMS loads the last 24 hours of Hotspots on the map ([nasa-firms.md](nasa-firms.md) checklist #1), Watch Duty reaches a useful answer at ≈zero clicks ([watch-duty.md](watch-duty.md) #1), Windy and ASMC follow the same shape. Meanwhile GitHub proves the calendar half can ship in the first HTML paint with zero config ([github-contrib-graph.md](github-contrib-graph.md) #1, #13). The anti-evidence is equally clear: GNW ex-GFW buries fire two menus under three default forest layers (~11 clicks to the first fire answer) and Windy hides its fire layer ~90 % down settings — a flagship view that is not on the first screen does not get used. The supplement adds two framing cautions: BMKG's homepage has zero fire content above the fold (product two menus deep), and NEA's hotspot canvas opens facing Myanmar with Borneo off-screen — IGNIS must open framed on the whole Coverage Area with all three countries labeled ([bmkg-fire-potential.md](bmkg-fire-potential.md) #1, B2; [nea-haze-portal.md](nea-haze-portal.md) #4). Co-locating when and where follows Climate Reanalyzer's chart-plus-map page. No audited app links a calendar and a map in both directions — that contract is IGNIS's own, and PATTERNS.md's Ground rules now specify it: clicking a calendar cell or a recent-days strip day filters the heatmap to that period; selecting an Area of Interest or a date on the heatmap recomputes and highlights the Burning Calendar; both share one URL state. On a phone the calendar lives in a bottom-sheet drawer with the recent-days strip always visible (per the *Mobile-first shell* pattern). Both views must land inside the Ground-rules performance budget — ≤ 3 s and ≤ 1 MB to first useful answer — the line FIRMS (0.5 MB) and SiPongi+ (~2.8 s) meet and GNW (12.5 s / 2 MB), ASMC (~18 s / 2.4 MB) and Worldview (15.6 s / 6.5 MB) fail. Patterns: *Map-first landing over the Coverage Area*, *Zoom-dependent Hotspot rendering: density, cluster, point*, *Year-by-month grid under a Typical Year*, *Flagship data one click from the top*, *Recent-days strip*, *Mobile-first shell with bottom-sheet drawers*.

### 2. Show raw MODIS and VIIRS layers, or only the Harmonized Hotspot Count?

**Recommendation: both — the Harmonized Hotspot Count is the default headline number and default layer at month-and-up cadence (Burning Calendar, region panel, rankings), while at now-cadence (last 24 h to ~15 days) the default layer is observed per-sensor Hotspots, since harmonization is fitted monthly (H(y,m) = k(m) × M(y,m)) and no harmonized daily number exists; per-sensor MODIS/VIIRS counts always visible beside the headline, and raw per-sensor layers one toggle away, each with a plain-language capability label.**

Evidence: FIRMS merges everything into one layer with no per-sensor toggles and its audit calls that its biggest gap ([nasa-firms.md](nasa-firms.md) #5); SiPongi+ and Watch Duty never merge at all — honest, but leaves no comparable series across the record, which is why GWIS can only juxtapose MODIS and VIIRS charts instead of reconciling them. GitHub shows the shape that works: one count in the cell, per-source breakdown one click down, method link under the grid. The Borneo supplement supplies the direct precedent and the sharpest cautionary tale at once: Nusantara Atlas publishes year comparisons that "use the same satellites in both years, avoiding an artificial increase caused by adding sensors" — exactly the footnote the Harmonized Hotspot Count needs wherever it appears ([nusantara-atlas.md](nusantara-atlas.md) #5, B4) — while BMKG's own two same-day counts diverge by confidence scope (26 high-confidence vs 784 all-confidence national) with no reconciliation, and BNPB's incident counts (30 for Kaltim, Aug 2026) sit unexplained beside SiPongi's thousands of satellite Hotspots ([bmkg-fire-potential.md](bmkg-fire-potential.md) B4; [bnpb-disaster-geoportal.md](bnpb-disaster-geoportal.md) B4). What the headline number *is* has been settled in the repo: the [harmonization analysis](../harmonization-analysis.md) and [ADR 0001](../../../docs/adr/0001-viirs-equivalent-scaling.md) define the Harmonized Hotspot Count as VIIRS-equivalent counts — observed VIIRS from 2012, MODIS-only 2002–2011 months scaled by per-month factors at ±12% — and the app should link straight to them wherever the number appears. Cadence is part of the answer: the *Recent-days strip* and the now half of the *Now-view vs record-view toggle* show observed per-sensor counts, never a fabricated daily harmonized number. Patterns: *Per-sensor counts beside the Harmonized Hotspot Count* (with the disagreement resolved toward GitHub's model), *Same-satellite discipline and the why-our-count-differs bridge*, *The Harmonized Hotspot Count block* (which assembles the number, split, method link and caveat into one renderable unit), *Sensor-explicit layers with capability one-liners and freshness stamps*, *Read-then-drill ladder*, *Methodology one click from every number*.

### 3. Calendar granularity: daily, weekly or monthly cells?

**Recommendation: monthly cells by default, with one-click re-binning radios to weekly and daily for drill-down.**

Evidence from the audited apps first: GWIS's month×year fire-season heatmap is the working ancestor — a whole multi-decade record reads in one glance ([gwis.md](gwis.md) walkthrough task 2), and monthly is the finest granularity that keeps a 25-year harmonized record (2002–present, per the [harmonization analysis](../harmonization-analysis.md)) on one screen. GitHub's daily grid works only because a year fits 53 slim columns and it still offers no aggregation ([github-contrib-graph.md](github-contrib-graph.md)); ASMC's monthly charts answer the same question but cost 5 clicks per year because nothing is linked ([asmc.md](asmc.md) task 2); the D3 notebooks prove re-binning is a one-click radio, not a zoom ([d3-calendar.md](d3-calendar.md) — facet radios rebuilt the chart in one verified click). GLOSSARY.md's definition of the Burning Calendar as a year-by-month grid is the team's codified conclusion from this same evidence, not independent support for it. Daily detail still matters for responders, delivered two ways: the drill-down (GitHub's cell-click filters a detail list) and the *Recent-days strip* pattern in PATTERNS.md (APIMS's hourly chip table, ASMC's 10-day chips, Windy's day strip). Nusantara Atlas independently confirms the split with cadence-matched presets — short windows for the daily Hotspot feed, month/multi-month windows for the monthly record ([nusantara-atlas.md](nusantara-atlas.md) #2). Patterns: *Year-by-month grid under a Typical Year*, *One-click re-binning radios*, *Cadence-matched time presets, season-to-date included*, *Read-then-drill ladder*, *Recent-days strip*.

### 4. Do we need a Sarawak haze/impact view, or only burning in the Coverage Area?

**Recommendation: yes to haze impact, as a panel inside the region panel rather than a separate view — current air-quality tier plus health actions beside the Harmonized Hotspot Count, anchored to the Typical Year.**

Evidence: the plan's own audience list includes the haze-impact audience — defined in PATTERNS.md as everyone in the Coverage Area exposed to haze, downwind Sarawak/Kuching and source-side Kalimantan alike — and the audit set shows exactly how to serve them: IQAir's Kuching page answers "how bad is the air" in 2 clicks with tier, WHO context and an action ([apims-iqair.md](apims-iqair.md) task 1); APIMS's tier cards answer "how bad overall, where do I sit" in 0 clicks; ASMC pairs its map with a daily prose narrative translating Hotspots into haze and air quality, plus wind/rain overlays. IQAir's map also demonstrates cause and consequence on one canvas ("Fires" beside "Wind" and air-quality stations). The supplement's NEA haze portal — the region's maturest public haze site — supplies the sentence template IGNIS should generate per Area of Interest (foreign source, transport wind, local tier, one action), the healthy-vs-vulnerable audience split on every advisory band, and the two-state portal precedent for promoting the alert block when conditions turn ([nea-haze-portal.md](nea-haze-portal.md) B2, patterns). Keeping it a panel — Burning Phase tier cards + AQI tier + health action + a "3× a Typical March" anchor — serves that audience without fragmenting the app the way ASMC's separate portal pages fragment theirs. One dependency is open and named as such in the pattern: no audited source exposes a free, downloadable Sarawak/Kuching air-quality feed (APIMS's pages are static with no export; IQAir publishes no public CSV; ASMC's WIS2 catalog covers Hotspot coordinates, not AQ; NEA delegates older PSI to Data.gov.sg, a Singapore dataset), so which feed ships — APIMS/MyEQMS, IQAir, or an ASMC tie-in — is an architect decision, and until it is settled the panel links out rather than fabricating a tier. Patterns: *Haze-impact panel with health actions and a Typical Year anchor*, *Burning Phase tier cards with counts and ranges*, *Quiet-state vs Peak-state home*, *One tabbed context-overlay panel*.

### 5. Which languages ship first?

**Recommendation: ship one string table at launch with English as the default plus Bahasa Indonesia and Bahasa Malaysia behind a one-click toggle in the header.**

Evidence: SiPongi+ proves a complete ID/EN toggle translating every string (nav, filters, dates, admin levels) is achievable for exactly this audience ([sipongi.md](sipongi.md) #12 — verdict inherit); GNW ex-GFW ships six languages including Bahasa Indonesia and its audit marks that inherit; APIMS publishes its haze advisory in Bahasa Malaysia, showing the demand on the Sarawak side. The avoid-side evidence: FIRMS, ASMC, GWIS, Worldview and Copernicus Browser are all English-only despite serving Borneo-adjacent audiences — GNW and SiPongi+ are the only Tier-1 apps that meet their audience in its own language. The Borneo supplement sharpens both sides: BMKG and BNPB are national authorities shipping BI-only — BMKG even for its ASEAN-wide product that spans four more official languages — NEA ships English chrome with four-language PDF guides but no Bahasa Indonesia at all, and Nusantara Atlas is English-only for a tri-country audience ([bmkg-fire-potential.md](bmkg-fire-potential.md) #12, B3; [bnpb-disaster-geoportal.md](bnpb-disaster-geoportal.md) #12, B3; [nea-haze-portal.md](nea-haze-portal.md) #12, B3; [nusantara-atlas.md](nusantara-atlas.md) #12, B3). Every single-language posture misses part of the Coverage Area. The Coverage Area spans Bahasa Indonesia-speaking Kalimantan (including the source-side residents the haze-impact audience definition covers) and Bahasa Malaysia-speaking Sarawak, Sabah and Brunei, so EN+ID+MS at launch is the minimum honest scope; the *combination* is IGNIS's own, so the pattern's verdict is adapt (mechanism inherited from SiPongi+, scope added). Pattern: *English + Bahasa Indonesia + Bahasa Malaysia from day one, Iban next*.

Iban is a team addition, not something the audits found: it is the language of Sarawak's largest ethnic group and reaches rural and longhouse communities near recurring burning. No audited app ships it and the fire terms lack settled Iban wording, so build the string table for a fourth locale now and ship Iban at launch only if a native-speaker reviewer checks the strings; otherwise it is the first post-launch locale.

### 6. Supplement Q1 — Default landing view for a tri-country audience?

**Recommendation: open on the whole Coverage Area — the full Borneo extent, all three countries drawn, labeled and equally treated — with no country's framing leading; optionally offer the visitor's geo-located country as a one-click zoom chip after first paint, and restore an Area of Interest only through the shared URL state (a shared link always wins over geolocation).**

Evidence: Nusantara Atlas proves a Borneo-wide extent is shippable — its About page documents analysis "anywhere in Indonesia, Malaysian Borneo and Brunei" — but also shows the trap: its fire dashboards are Indonesia-first inside that map, leaving Sarawak/Sabah as unlabeled context ([nusantara-atlas.md](nusantara-atlas.md) B2). BMKG's ASEAN map is the inverse trap: it colors all three countries but labels none of them, under Indonesian national branding, and its Kalimantan map greys out the non-Indonesian side ([bmkg-fire-potential.md](bmkg-fire-potential.md) B2). NEA is own-country-first done well — for a national portal with national PSI regions; IGNIS is not a national portal, and NEA's own hotspot canvas (which opens facing away from Borneo) shows where own-country framing fails a regional audience ([nea-haze-portal.md](nea-haze-portal.md) #1, #4). BNPB is the strictly-national shell IGNIS differs from by design ([bnpb-disaster-geoportal.md](bnpb-disaster-geoportal.md) B2). About/method copy follows the map: frame everything as the Coverage Area (GLOSSARY.md's own term), no flag or country leads, and the three countries' names appear together on the first screen — the honest default for an audience split across all of them. Patterns: *Map-first landing over the Coverage Area*, *Cross-border comparison with equal country standing*, *Tri-country coded Areas of Interest at mixed depths*.

### 7. Supplement Q2 — Which region presets ship first, and at what boundary depth?

**Recommendation: depth-1 presets at launch — the whole-Coverage-Area rollup plus the 5 Kalimantan provinces, Sarawak, Sabah, W.P. Labuan and Brunei's 4 daerah, each labeled with its own country's level name (Provinsi / State / Daerah) — then depth 2 (kabupaten/kota for Kalimantan, Sarawak divisions, Sabah districts) in the same picker once tri-country boundary GIS is confirmed, always selecting and storing official admin codes rather than names. One data-scope caveat stated up front: at launch the Indonesian presets carry per-province Harmonized Hotspot Counts and Typical Years, but the Malaysian/Bruneian presets ship as selectable geography plus observed Hotspots until the harmonization refit — the repo's own record holds Sarawak + Sabah + Brunei as one approximate region, so their per-AOI Burning Calendars do not exist yet.**

Evidence: BMKG's searchable 42-preset ladder — all five Kalimantan provinces plus island/national/ASEAN rollups — is the audited shape of a depth-1-plus-rollup picker, and its ASEAN map proves the underlying data already spans Sarawak/Sabah/Brunei ([bmkg-fire-potential.md](bmkg-fire-potential.md) #3, B1, B2). BNPB's Kemendagri codes (`id_prov`/`id_kab` on every record) are the coded-storage model that survives the boundary changes GWIS's province list still trips over — its list predates the 2012 Kalimantan Utara split ([bnpb-disaster-geoportal.md](bnpb-disaster-geoportal.md) B1; [gwis.md](gwis.md) caveats). Where deep boundary GIS does not yet exist, Nusantara Atlas shows flattening to one depth across all three countries is an acceptable v1 ([nusantara-atlas.md](nusantara-atlas.md) B1), and GNW ex-GFW's "Political boundaries" preset layer is the audited proof tri-country boundary GIS exists as a product to integrate ([global-forest-watch.md](global-forest-watch.md) #3). Labuan ships as a named federal-territory preset even though it is tiny — it completes Malaysia's Borneo footprint and costs one row; ASMC's preset set (Kalimantan, Sabah & Sarawak, Brunei…) is the chart-side confirmation that these are the region names the region already uses ([asmc.md](asmc.md) #3).

Data honesty, because the preset list is not as data-backed as it looks: the [harmonization analysis](../harmonization-analysis.md) splits its Borneo record into the five Kalimantan provinces plus one Sarawak + Sabah + Brunei region that is explicitly approximate ("every Hotspot north of 0.8°N … that falls outside an Indonesian province"), warns that "a Burning Calendar fitted on West Kalimantan does not describe Malaysian Borneo" (the MY/BN side has no sharp season — March to September each carry 7–19%, versus West Kalimantan's 70% in August–September), and lists as its first limitation: "Sarawak, Sabah and Brunei are still one approximate region; add Malaysian and Bruneian boundaries to split them and refit". So the MY/BN presets select and show observed Hotspots immediately, but their per-AOI Harmonized Hotspot Counts, Typical Years and Burning Phases — including any Peak classification (see the *Burning Phase encoding* cutoff caveat) — arrive with that refit; until then those views render at the approximate-region scope, labeled as such on the chart. The same honesty applies to codes: Kemendagri ids are the only official code system any audit touched, and Malaysia's and Brunei's equivalents are unaudited — both are registered dependencies in PATTERNS.md's Open dependencies register. Patterns: *Tri-country coded Areas of Interest at mixed depths*, *Named Areas of Interest: click-a-shape, presets, draw*, *Cross-border comparison with equal country standing* (whose buildable mechanism and scope caveat this answer shares).

### 8. Supplement Q3 — Language rollout: which locales, whose place-name spellings, one glossary or two?

**Recommendation: EN + BM + BI in one string table at launch (per answer 5); place names anchored to each locale's endonyms (Kalimantan Barat in BI and BM, West Kalimantan in EN) from one coded gazetteer; separate BM and BI glossaries for fire vocabulary — BM api/jerebu, BI karhutla/titik panas, EN Hotspot — sharing one canonical term list derived from GLOSSARY.md; and Iban as the planned fourth locale per the committed team addition — its string table built now, shipped at launch only with native-speaker sign-off, otherwise first post-launch, with NEA-style downloadable guides as the stopgap for any locale that misses a release.**

Evidence: SiPongi+ proves the full-locale toggle mechanism ([sipongi.md](sipongi.md) #12); the divergent vocabularies are documented on all three sides — BNPB's official BI class "KEBAKARAN HUTAN DAN LAHAN" and BMKG's "titik panas", APIMS's BM "Nasihat Semasa Jerebu", NEA's English "hotspot" prose — and BNPB's page carrying two coexisting disaster-class vocabularies ("LETUSAN GUNUNG API" vs "ERUPSI GUNUNG API") is the live demonstration of why one canonical, coded glossary per locale is needed rather than ad-hoc translation ([bnpb-disaster-geoportal.md](bnpb-disaster-geoportal.md) #12, B3; [bmkg-fire-potential.md](bmkg-fire-potential.md) #12; [apims-iqair.md](apims-iqair.md) #9; [nea-haze-portal.md](nea-haze-portal.md) #12). NEA's four-language advisory PDFs show the artifact pattern works for actions while its missing Bahasa Indonesia edition marks exactly the source-side audience it fails ([nea-haze-portal.md](nea-haze-portal.md) #12, B3). Iban, per the committed addition preserved in answer 5 and in PATTERNS.md: no audited app ships it and the GLOSSARY.md terms (Hotspot, Harmonized Hotspot Count, Burning Phase) lack settled Iban wording, so the fourth locale is planned now, gated on a native-speaker reviewer, and weighed here as the first post-launch locale unless that reviewer signs off before launch. Patterns: *English + Bahasa Indonesia + Bahasa Malaysia from day one, Iban next*, *Tri-country coded Areas of Interest at mixed depths* (the coded gazetteer that carries localized place names). The gazetteer and the Iban reviewer are registered — with their gatees — in PATTERNS.md's Open dependencies register, so neither reads as settled.
