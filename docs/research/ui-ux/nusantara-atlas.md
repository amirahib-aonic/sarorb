## Summary
Nusantara Atlas (by The TreeMap / SARL SilviaMap) is an open-access platform monitoring deforestation, fires, peatland degradation and forest regeneration in Equatorial Asia: a Google-Maps-based interactive map of concessions, land use and peat with time-lapse satellite imagery, a stories blog, and a new FireWatch fire-season dashboard pairing daily VIIRS/MODIS fire hotspots with independent monthly Sentinel-2 burn-scar maps. For IGNIS's tri-country Coverage Area its decisive contribution is the answer the audit was asked to confirm: the map extent is **not** Indonesia-only — the About page states you can "visualize and analyze temporal trends in deforestation and fires anywhere in Indonesia, Malaysian Borneo and Brunei down to municipalities" — though the fire record and FireWatch analytics are documented as Indonesia-national only. Its second gift is count discipline: year comparisons explicitly "use the same satellites in both years, avoiding an artificial increase caused by adding sensors" — a direct precedent for IGNIS's Harmonized Hotspot Count.

## Patterns worth taking
- **Sensor-consistent year comparison**: every published year-vs-year count states "these comparisons use the same satellites in both years, avoiding an artificial increase caused by adding sensors", with a second consistent-sensor-since-2015 check to expose drift. For IGNIS: any trend over the Overlap Period must say which sensor set produced it, and the Harmonized Hotspot Count needs exactly this footnote. Serves the Scientist.
- **Detections + impact as a documented pair**: daily VIIRS/MODIS hotspots ("active fires as point locations") paired with monthly Sentinel-2 burn scars (hectares), plus the standing explainer that "hotspots and burned area measure different aspects of the fire season". For IGNIS: pair the Hotspot record with a burned-area context layer and say in one sentence why they differ. Serves all three users.
- **Land-unit Alert Tracker**: ranked lists of concessions, protected areas and jurisdictions most affected by fire or deforestation over the selected period, each with a View button that zooms the map and overlays alerts for verification against satellite imagery. For IGNIS: the region panel as ranked, actionable lists — not just a boundary picker. Serves First responder + Land manager.
- **Per-land-unit fire-history dashboard**: for any concession, protected area or jurisdiction, dashboards summarise "worst year, least-affected year, current situation and average" across eight graphs. For IGNIS: this is the Burning Calendar's ancestor — Typical Year plus unusual years for a chosen Area of Interest. Serves the Land manager.
- **Cadence-matched time presets**: Today / Last 2 / 7 / 14 Days for the daily indicator; Last Month / 3 / 6 / 12 Months plus custom ≤360 days for the monthly one. For IGNIS: one preset row per cadence (latest Hotspots vs Burning Calendar history) instead of one generic date picker. Serves First responder + Scientist.
- **Citation-per-layer methodology block**: the About page lists peer-reviewed citations per dataset (Hansen, Margono, Gaveau, GLAD/RADD, Schroeder VIIRS, Justice MODIS) and the burn-scar method by journal and processor. For IGNIS: every layer and the harmonization formula get a citation on the method page. Serves the Scientist.
- **Uncertainty as a range across official maps**: burned-area results published as ranges keyed to source-map vintage (17–23% inside peat boundaries depending on the 2011 vs 2019 MoA maps; 85k vs 88k ha forest across two baselines), with plain-language caveats against over-reading overlap. For IGNIS: show what the answer becomes under the neighbouring official dataset. Serves the Scientist + Land manager.
- **Land-use overlay attribution on fire impacts**: burned hectares attributed to what burned — 59,200 ha in planted oil palm, 16,300 ha in industrial Acacia, shares by land-cover class and peat overlap. For IGNIS: the heatmap's where-context — recurring Hotspots explained by peatland, concession and land-use layers in one stack. Serves all three users.
- **Whole-island extent with honest depth asymmetry**: one map covering Indonesia, Malaysian Borneo and Brunei, while stating (implicitly, by scope of its dashboards) that the fire record runs Indonesia-only. For IGNIS: default the Coverage Area to the whole island, and label per-country data depth explicitly rather than letting the dashboard imply it. Serves all three users (and answers the supplement's Q1).
- **Cautionary patterns (avoid)**: an English-only UI for a tri-country audience; a marketing homepage + full-screen spinner before any data; Indonesia-first "national" dashboards inside a Borneo-wide map; hotspot history capped at short presets (custom ranges documented only for burn scars, ≤360 days); no documented CSV/API export from the map itself. For IGNIS: none of these survive contact with the Coverage Area.
